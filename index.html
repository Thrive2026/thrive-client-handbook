Section 01

Welcome to Thrive

Welcome! We're glad you're here.

At Thrive, we believe in your ability to live a meaningful, fulfilling life in recovery. Whether you're receiving outpatient treatment, case management services, or working with a Peer Recovery Specialist, our team is here to support you in achieving your personal goals.

This handbook will help you understand

  • Our three service lines and what each provides
  • What you can expect during your time with us
  • How we'll work together toward your goals
  • Important guidelines that keep everyone safe
  • Service limitations and timelines
  • Your rights as a client

We're excited to partner with you on your recovery journey.

Section 02

Understanding Our Services

Thrive offers three distinct service lines to support your recovery journey. Each service is designed to meet different needs and provide specific types of support.

At a glance: our service lines

Outpatient Treatment

What it is

Clinical therapy services provided by licensed professionals

Who provides it

Licensed mental health professionals

Best for

Addressing mental health or substance use challenges through individual therapy

Case Management

What it is

Coordination and navigation support to connect you with community resources and services

Who provides it

Trained case managers

Best for

Navigating systems, accessing resources, and coordinating multiple services

Peer Support

What it is

Support from someone with lived recovery experience who walks alongside you on your journey

Who provides it

Certified Peer Recovery Specialists with personal recovery experience

Best for

Building recovery skills, connecting with community, and receiving hope-based support

How Services Are Funded

Thrive services are primarily funded through Medicaid. We also have a few other funding sources available. Your provider can discuss payment and coverage options with you.

Thrive does not currently operate a self-pay program, so if your services are covered by Medicaid or another funding source, you should not receive a bill from us. If your coverage ever changes, or if Thrive adds self-pay or sliding-fee-scale options in the future, we'll explain any fees and payment options clearly before you're billed for anything.

Federal law (the No Surprises Act) protects patients from unexpected medical bills, including the right to a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges if you're ever uninsured or paying out of pocket. Because Thrive doesn't currently bill clients directly, this typically won't apply to your care with us — but ask your provider if you have questions about costs.

Section 03

Outpatient Treatment Services

What Is Outpatient Treatment?

Outpatient treatment provides clinical therapy services from licensed mental health professionals. This structured support helps you address mental health challenges, substance use concerns, and develop healthy coping strategies through individual therapy sessions.

Provides
  • Individual therapy sessions with licensed providers
  • Evidence-based treatment approaches (CBT, DBT, motivational interviewing, etc.)
  • Treatment planning and clinical assessments
  • Coordination with other healthcare providers
  • Progress monitoring and outcome measurement
Does not provide
  • 24/7 crisis response or crisis intervention services
  • Group therapy
  • Medication prescribing (you'll need a psychiatrist or medical provider)
  • Inpatient or residential treatment
  • Medical detoxification services
  • Transportation or housing assistance

Service Expectations & Timelines

Session frequency

  • Determined by your individual needs, medical necessity, and treatment plan
  • Sessions are typically 45–60 minutes
  • Your provider will work with you to determine the right frequency

Progress reviews

  • Progress reviewed regularly with your provider and/or a member of the clinical team
  • Treatment plans reviewed and updated at least every 90 days

Cancellation policy

  • Please provide 24 hours notice for cancellations when possible
  • Multiple no-shows may affect your continued services

Returning to services

If you complete services and later feel you need support again, you can reconnect with us. You will be assessed for need and appropriateness of services.

Service discontinuation — treatment may conclude when

  • You've achieved your treatment goals
  • You request to end services
  • You miss appointments for 60 days despite outreach attempts
  • You need a different level of care (we'll help connect you)
  • Your behavior compromises the safety of the agency or provider
Section 04

Case Management Services

What Is Case Management?

Case management helps you navigate complex systems, access community resources, and coordinate the various services you need. Your case manager serves as your advocate and guide in connecting with support.

Provides
  • Assessment of your needs and available resources
  • Connection to community services (housing, employment, healthcare, etc.)
  • Coordination between multiple service providers
  • Assistance with applications and paperwork
  • Advocacy and support in navigating systems
  • Regular follow-up to ensure services are meeting your needs
  • Crisis planning and problem-solving
Does not provide
  • Clinical therapy
  • Crisis intervention services
  • Direct financial assistance
  • Transportation services
  • Housing placement (we help you connect to housing resources)
  • Legal services or medical care
  • 24/7 availability

Service Expectations & Timelines

Contact frequency

  • Determined by your individual needs, medical necessity, and service plan
  • Minimum of bi-weekly contact (every two weeks) for most clients
  • Contact may be in-person, by phone, or virtual

Response times

  • Routine inquiries: 1–2 business days
  • Urgent (non-emergency) matters: within 1 business day
  • Crisis situations: contact Mobile Crisis or 911

Progress reviews

  • Progress reviewed regularly with your case manager and/or a member of the clinical team
  • Service plans reviewed and updated at least every 90 days

Returning to services

If you complete services and later feel you need support again, you can reconnect with us. You will be assessed for need and appropriateness of services.

Service discontinuation — case management may conclude when

  • You're successfully connected to needed services
  • You're able to independently navigate systems
  • You request to end services
  • You don't respond to contact for 60 days (after three attempts)
  • Your behavior compromises the safety of the agency or provider
Section 05

Peer Support Services

What Is Peer Support?

Peer support is a unique service built on the foundation of shared lived experience. Your Peer Recovery Specialist (PRS) is someone who has navigated their own recovery journey and is now trained to support you in yours.

Core principles of peer support

You are in charge

This is your recovery journey. You decide what goals matter to you, what support you need, and what steps you want to take.

It's all about relationships

The relationship between you and your Peer Recovery Specialist is the foundation of everything we do.

It's completely voluntary

You choose to participate. You can take breaks, adjust frequency, or end services at any time.

Community-centered

We believe relationships and social connections are essential to recovery.

Recovery-oriented

Recovery looks different for everyone. We support multiple pathways to wellness.

Trauma-informed

We create a safe space where you feel respected, empowered, and in control.

Provides
  • Shared lived experience and hope
  • Support in identifying and working toward your goals
  • Connection to community resources
  • Life skills development
  • Building natural support systems
  • Celebration of progress and support through challenges
  • Advocacy and system navigation support
Does not provide
  • Clinical therapy
  • Medical advice or treatment
  • Crisis intervention services
  • 24/7 availability
  • Financial assistance or transportation
  • Decisions about your recovery (you're always in charge)

Service Expectations & Timelines

Contact frequency

  • Determined by your individual needs, medical necessity, and treatment plan
  • Minimum weekly contact for most clients (phone, text, or in-person/virtual meeting)
  • Flexible scheduling based on your needs

Response times

  • Non-urgent messages: 1–2 business days
  • Crisis situations: contact Mobile Crisis or 911

Progress reviews

  • Progress reviewed regularly with your PRS and/or a member of the clinical team
  • Treatment plans reviewed and updated at least every 90 days

Typical assignment

  • One Peer Recovery Specialist per client (for consistency)
  • In special circumstances (major transitions), a second PRS may be temporarily assigned

Graduation — you may be ready when

  • You've achieved your treatment plan goals
  • You have a strong support system in place
  • You feel confident managing your recovery independently
  • You no longer need this level of support

Service discontinuation — services may conclude if

  • You've successfully graduated
  • You request to end services
  • No response for 3 consecutive weeks (we'll reach out to check in)
  • No contact for 60 days after three outreach attempts
  • You need a different level of care
  • Your behavior compromises the safety of the agency or provider

Recovery isn't linear. If you complete services and later feel you need support again, you can reconnect with us. You will be assessed for need and appropriateness of services.

Section 06

Mutual Respect and Professional Boundaries

Applies to all services

Our Mutual Respect Agreement

At Thrive, we believe in treating everyone with dignity and respect. We have a mutual respect agreement that guides our interactions:

  • We commit to treating you with respect, compassion, and professionalism
  • We ask that you treat our staff with the same respect
  • We maintain a safe environment for everyone
  • Behaviors that compromise safety will result in service discontinuation

Professional Boundaries We Maintain

To maintain healthy, professional relationships that serve your recovery, all Thrive staff follow these important boundaries:

  • Meeting locations

    We meet in locations that are safe and comfortable for both you and your provider. This may include community locations, our office, your home (when appropriate), or via secure telehealth. Meeting location decisions are based on safety and comfort of both client and provider.

  • No money exchange

    We don't give, lend, or accept money.

  • No gift exchange

    We can't exchange gifts to maintain professional boundaries.

  • Social media

    We don't connect on personal social media during active services.

  • Consistent service

    We provide the same quality of care to all clients.

  • Professional focus

    We keep our relationship focused on your recovery goals.

These boundaries protect you and ensure our relationship stays focused on supporting your recovery.

Telehealth Consent

Some Thrive services may be offered by secure telehealth (video or phone) instead of in person. By participating in telehealth, you're agreeing to a few things:

  • Telehealth carries the same privacy protections as in-person care, but you're responsible for finding a private location on your end where others can't overhear your session
  • As with any technology, there's a small risk of technical failure or interruption; if that happens, your provider will follow up to reschedule or reconnect
  • You can request in-person services instead of telehealth at any time, without it affecting your care
  • You'll need a private, stable internet or phone connection and a device that supports video or audio calls

Your provider will review telehealth-specific consent with you before your first virtual session.

Section 07

Your Privacy and Confidentiality

Keeping your information safe.

Your privacy is extremely important to us. Everything you share is confidential and protected under federal law (HIPAA).

What this means

  • We won't share information about you without your written permission
  • Your records are kept secure and private
  • We only discuss your case with our clinical team as needed for your care
  • We won't acknowledge you in public unless you acknowledge us first

When We Must Share Information (Legal Exceptions)

1

Safety concerns

If you're planning to seriously hurt yourself or someone else, we must take action to keep everyone safe.

2

Child abuse or neglect

If a child is being abused or neglected, we must report it to Children Services.

3

Elder or dependent adult abuse

If an elderly person (60+) or dependent adult is being abused, we must report it.

4

Court orders

If a court orders us to share your records, we must comply. For substance use treatment records specifically, federal law requires a special court order that meets additional legal standards — a routine subpoena is not enough on its own (see 42 CFR Part 2, below).

Extra Protection for Substance Use Treatment Records (42 CFR Part 2)

Because Thrive receives federal and Medicaid funding and provides substance use diagnosis, treatment, or referral, your substance use treatment records are protected by a federal law called 42 CFR Part 2 — in addition to HIPAA. Part 2 is stricter than HIPAA in several ways:

  • We generally cannot disclose that you are receiving substance use treatment at Thrive, or share any related records, without your specific written consent — even to other medical providers, family members, or insurers
  • Any information we're permitted to share comes with a legal notice prohibiting the recipient from redisclosing it further without your separate consent
  • Your substance use records generally cannot be used against you in a criminal investigation or prosecution without a special court order
  • These protections continue to apply even if you stop receiving services at Thrive

The legal exceptions listed above (safety concerns, child abuse or neglect, elder/dependent adult abuse) still apply. If you have questions about how Part 2 affects your specific situation, ask your provider — they can walk through what this means for you.

Sharing Information With Your Permission

To coordinate with other providers, you'll sign a Release of Information (ROI) form specifying:

  • Who we can share information with
  • What type of information we can share
  • How long the authorization lasts

You can cancel this permission at any time.

Section 08

Crisis Support and Safety

Getting help when you need it most.

Crisis resources
Suicide & Crisis LifelineCall or text 988
Mobile Crisis TeamCall or text 988
EmergencyCall 911
Available 24/7

Thrive serves clients across multiple Ohio counties, and the local Mobile Crisis Team differs by county. Calling or texting 988 connects you with Ohio's Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, which coordinates with the mobile crisis team for your area — you don't need to know the number in advance. Your provider will also give you the direct Mobile Crisis contact for your county at the start of services.

Important: Thrive does not provide crisis intervention services or 24/7 crisis response. For emergencies, always contact Mobile Crisis, call 988, or call 911.

How Thrive Can Help With Crisis Situations

While we don't provide crisis intervention, we can:

  • Assist you in contacting Mobile Crisis or other crisis resources
  • Support you in creating a personal safety plan
  • Follow up with you after a crisis to support your ongoing recovery
  • Help you strengthen coping skills and identify warning signs
Section 09

Your Rights and Responsibilities

Language Access and Interpreter Services

Thrive provides free interpreter and translation services for clients who need them, including American Sign Language and support in your preferred spoken language. This is available for sessions, phone calls, and written materials on request — just let your provider or our office know, and we'll arrange it. You should never have to rely on a family member or friend to interpret for you unless you choose to.

Your rights as a client

  • Be free from abuse, neglect, and exploitation by anyone, including Thrive staff
  • Be treated with dignity, respect, and compassion
  • Receive services regardless of race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, or gender identity
  • Receive free interpreter services and materials in your preferred language
  • Make your own decisions about your recovery
  • Refuse any service or support without penalty
  • Privacy and confidentiality (with legal exceptions noted)
  • Access your treatment records
  • Know who is providing your care
  • Voice concerns or complaints without fear of retaliation
  • Request a different provider if the relationship isn't working
  • End services at any time
  • Participate in creating your treatment plan

Your responsibilities

  • Communicate openly about your needs
  • Let us know if you need to cancel or reschedule
  • Treat staff with respect
  • Work toward your treatment goals
  • Let us know if something isn't working
  • Respect professional boundaries
  • Provide feedback to help us improve
  • Maintain a safe environment by treating others with respect

Requesting a Different Provider

If the relationship isn't working, you can request a different provider. When you request a change, we will inquire about your reasons to better ensure appropriateness of the match and improve our services.

  • Contact the appropriate director or supervisor
  • Reassignment completed as soon as possible
  • Services continue without interruption

Advance Directives

You have the right to create an advance directive — a legal document that states your wishes for care if you're ever unable to speak for yourself. This includes a psychiatric advance directive (PAD), which lets you document your preferences for mental health treatment, medications, and hospitalization in advance, and name someone you trust to make decisions on your behalf.

Thrive doesn't require you to have an advance directive, and we won't condition services on whether you do. If you already have one, let your provider know so we can keep a copy in your file and honor it. If you'd like help creating one, ask your provider — they can point you to Ohio-specific forms and resources.

Research and Experimental Procedures

Thrive is not currently conducting any research studies or offering experimental treatment. If that ever changes, you would be fully informed about the research, its purpose, and any risks or benefits, and asked to give separate, written, voluntary consent before participating. Declining to participate in research never affects your access to regular services.
